Five questions

Five questions that will change how you think about business.

Remember these. They are worth more than every date, name and anecdote from a founder's life.

  1. Focus

    If I could only work on one thing for the next 12 months, what would create the biggest result?

  2. Value

    What problem do people have that I can solve 10 times better?

  3. Simplicity

    What am I doing that I could eliminate?

  4. Excellence

    What would I improve if my reputation depended on every single detail?

  5. Innovation

    What does everyone in my industry accept as normal that I could challenge?

The blueprint

Eleven principles. One test: does the company work for the customer, or for itself?

Every card in the Corporate BS Detector tests one or two of these. They come from the lessons of Steve Jobs's career, translated into things you can observe at work on a Tuesday.

See all eleven principles
PrincipleThe questionWhat a bureaucracy does
FocusA few meaningful priorities, or a growing pile?Adds priorities and never removes one.
SimplicityIs complexity removed on purpose, or added by default?Adds a step so it can be tracked.
Customer obsessionDo decisions start with the customer, or with internal convenience?Optimizes for the department's convenience.
ExcellenceAre quality and detail valued, even where nobody is watching?Meets the spec and calls it done.
InnovationCan people challenge the conventional and try new things?Forms a working group.
TalentAre exceptional people hired, trusted and given real responsibility?Hires to fill seats and promotes by tenure.
OwnershipIs every outcome owned, or does responsibility dissolve?Shares it across five departments.
SpeedCan an idea become action without needless delay?Routes it through the next committee.
IntegrationDo departments deliver one end-to-end customer experience?Lets every department hit its own target.
CourageWill leaders challenge what exists and reinvent the business?Defends the profitable thing that is fading.
Human leadershipHigh standards, with respect for the people meeting them?Confuses pressure with standards, or comfort with respect.

Structure is not the enemy. Financial controls, safety procedures, compliance and governance are part of a healthy company, and the game scores them that way. The aim is to remove structure that creates no value. The aim is not to copy the man. Aggression, intimidation, impossible deadlines and perfectionism that harms people are not on this list. High standards only count when people are treated with respect.

Build a company that works for the customer, not a bureaucracy that works for itself.

The difference

Same industry. Same quarter. Different company.

Nobody sets out to build a bureaucracy. It accumulates, like lint. Here is how to tell which one you are running.
