Five questions

Five questions Jobs kept asking.

Tap one to see the story behind it.

  1. FocusIf I could only work on one thing for the next 12 months, what would create the biggest result?

    In 1997 Jobs collapsed Apple's lineup into four boxes on a whiteboard. Saying no was the strategy.

    What bureaucracy says: “We cannot pick. Everything is tier one.”

  2. CustomerWhat problem do people have that I can solve 10 times better?

    Jobs started with the experience a person should have, then worked back to the technology.

    What bureaucracy says: “Ten percent better, with a roadmap and a launch event.”

  3. SimplicityWhat am I doing that I could eliminate?

    “1,000 songs in your pocket.” Making complex things simple takes extraordinary work, which is why most companies skip it.

    What bureaucracy says: “Let us add a step so we can track it.”

  4. ExcellenceWhat would I improve if my reputation depended on every single detail?

    His father taught him to finish the back of a cabinet nobody would see. Jobs held the insides of the Macintosh to the same standard.

    What bureaucracy says: “That is within tolerance.”

  5. InnovationWhat does everyone in my industry accept as normal that I could challenge?

    Some limits are real. Many are just old habits with good posture.

    What bureaucracy says: “That is how it is done here.”

The blueprint

Eleven principles. One test: does the company work for the customer, or for itself?

Every card tests one or two of these. Tap any to see what a bureaucracy does instead.

  • FocusA few meaningful priorities, or a growing pile?

    A bureaucracy: Adds priorities and never removes one.

  • SimplicityIs complexity removed on purpose, or added by default?

    A bureaucracy: Adds a step so it can be tracked.

  • Customer obsessionDo decisions start with the customer, or with internal convenience?

    A bureaucracy: Optimizes for the department's convenience.

  • ExcellenceAre quality and detail valued, even where nobody is watching?

    A bureaucracy: Meets the spec and calls it done.

  • InnovationCan people challenge the conventional and try new things?

    A bureaucracy: Forms a working group.

  • TalentAre exceptional people hired, trusted and given real responsibility?

    A bureaucracy: Hires to fill seats and promotes by tenure.

  • OwnershipIs every outcome owned, or does responsibility dissolve?

    A bureaucracy: Shares it across five departments.

  • SpeedCan an idea become action without needless delay?

    A bureaucracy: Routes it through the next committee.

  • IntegrationDo departments deliver one end-to-end customer experience?

    A bureaucracy: Lets every department hit its own target.

  • CourageWill leaders challenge what exists and reinvent the business?

    A bureaucracy: Defends the profitable thing that is fading.

  • Human leadershipHigh standards, with respect for the people meeting them?

    A bureaucracy: Confuses pressure with standards, or comfort with respect.

Never reward cruelty. Structure is not the enemy. Financial controls, safety procedures, compliance and governance are part of a healthy company, and the game scores them that way. The aim is to remove structure that creates no value. The aim is not to copy the man. Aggression, intimidation, impossible deadlines and perfectionism that harms people are not on this list. High standards only count when people are treated with respect.

Build a company that works for the customer, not a bureaucracy that works for itself.
