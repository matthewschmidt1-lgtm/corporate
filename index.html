The difference

Same industry. Same quarter. Different company.

Nobody sets out to build a bureaucracy. It accumulates, like lint, and underneath it is usually a company that forgot why it was built.

Five questions

Five questions in the spirit of Jobs.

Scroll. Each one opens as you reach it.

  1. FocusIf I could only work on one thing for the next 12 months, what would create the biggest result?

    In 1997 Jobs collapsed Apple's lineup into four boxes on a whiteboard. Saying no was the strategy.

    Monday. Write down the three things eating most of your time. Cut two.

  2. CustomerWhat problem do people have that I can solve 10 times better?

    Jobs started with the experience a person should have, then worked back to the technology.

    Monday. Name one frustrating problem your customers have. Make it disappear.

  3. SimplicityWhat am I doing that I could eliminate?

    “1,000 songs in your pocket.” Making complex things simple takes extraordinary work, which is why most companies skip it.

    Monday. Pick one process. Cut one step from it.

  4. ExcellenceWhat would I improve if my reputation depended on every single detail?

    His father taught him to finish the back of a cabinet nobody would see. Jobs held the insides of the Macintosh to the same standard.

    Monday. Check what customers touch: reply speed, presentation, ease of buying. Fix one.

  5. InnovationWhat does everyone in my industry accept as normal that I could challenge?

    Some limits are real. Many are just old habits with good posture.

    Monday. List three things your industry treats as law. Ask: real limit, or old habit?

The blueprint

Eleven principles. One chain.

Each one leads to the next. Tap through, and every card in the game tests one or two of them.

01 of 11

Focus

A few meaningful priorities, or a growing pile?

A bureaucracyAdds priorities and never removes one.

Saying no leaves less to do. Now make what is left simple.

02 of 11

Simplicity

Is complexity removed on purpose, or added by default?

A bureaucracyAdds a step so it can be tracked.

Simple for whom? Start with the person using it.

03 of 11

Customer obsession

Do decisions start with the customer, or with internal convenience?

A bureaucracyOptimizes for the department's convenience.

Then sweat the details they will feel, even the ones they cannot see.

04 of 11

Excellence

Are quality and detail valued, even where nobody is watching?

A bureaucracyMeets the spec and calls it done.

Excellent is not enough if you accept what everyone accepts. Question it.

05 of 11

Innovation

Can people challenge the conventional and try new things?

A bureaucracyForms a working group.

None of it happens without exceptional people.

06 of 11

Talent

Are exceptional people hired, trusted and given real responsibility?

A bureaucracyHires to fill seats and promotes by tenure.

Give them something to own. One outcome, one name.

07 of 11

Ownership

Is every outcome owned, or does responsibility dissolve?

A bureaucracyShares it across five departments.

Owners have to be able to move without asking permission.

08 of 11

Speed

Can an idea become action without needless delay?

A bureaucracyRoutes it through the next committee.

Speed breaks at the seams between departments. Own the whole experience.

09 of 11

Integration

Do departments deliver one end-to-end customer experience?

A bureaucracyLets every department hit its own target.

A working experience gets copied. Be willing to replace your own success.

10 of 11

Courage

Will leaders challenge what exists and reinvent the business?

A bureaucracyDefends the profitable thing that is fading.

And do all of it with respect for the people doing the work.

11 of 11

Human leadership

High standards, with respect for the people meeting them?

A bureaucracyConfuses pressure with standards, or comfort with respect.

That is the blueprint.
